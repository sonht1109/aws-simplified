How to Land an AWS DevOps Job

Five practical tips for getting hired: what order to learn things in, where certifications fit, how to read job descriptions, how to pick a course and why hands-on practice matters most.

Many people study AWS for months and still struggle in interviews. Usually they don't lack effort. They learn things in the wrong order, chase the wrong goals, or only watch and never build. The five tips below fix those problems.

Tip 1: Start with DevOps, not with AWS

A common mistake is to open the AWS console on day one and work through service after service. Without DevOps basics, AWS feels abstract. You create a VPC or an EKS cluster without knowing what you'll run on it or why. Many people find it too hard and give up.

Get a broad understanding of the popular DevOps tools first:

  • Linux and shell scripting: almost every server you touch runs Linux. (See Lesson 35 for a starter script.)
  • Git: version control for all code and configuration.
  • Docker and Kubernetes: how modern apps are packaged and run.
  • Terraform and Ansible: creating and configuring infrastructure with code.

Then move on to AWS. AWS services will make more sense once you know what problems they solve. EKS is "Kubernetes that AWS runs for you", and CodePipeline is "a CI/CD tool built into AWS".

TipYou may need AWS early anyway, for example to launch a Linux virtual machine if your laptop runs Windows. That's fine. Using one EC2 instance as a practice box is different from trying to master AWS first.

Tip 2: Certifications come last

Beginners often start by studying for an exam such as Cloud Practitioner or Solutions Architect Associate. Certifications are not useless. But they check knowledge you already have, and they can't replace building that knowledge.

A better order:

  1. Learn the concepts, tools and technologies.
  2. Do the demos and practicals yourself.
  3. Study interview questions and prepare for interviews.
  4. If you have time, take a certification to test what you know.
Think of it like…A driving test. Passing it shows you can drive, but you learn to drive by driving, not by memorising the test. An interviewer cares whether you can do the job.

Tip 3: Study job descriptions

DevOps and AWS are both huge. You can't learn everything, so let the job market tell you what matters. Spend 15–20 minutes a day reading job descriptions (JDs) instead of only scrolling your feed.

  • Collect many JDs (around 100 is a good target) for the roles you want.
  • Write down the tools and skills that keep coming up. AI chat tools can help you summarise them.
  • Compare that list with what you know. Every gap is your next thing to study.

Don't copy someone else's path just because a LinkedIn post said a certain certificate got them hired. Base your preparation on the jobs you're applying for.

Tip 4: Pick the right course, and follow it in order

There are courses on YouTube, Udemy and many other platforms. Look for courses that are built around jobs: real projects and the questions interviewers ask, not trivia or exam tricks. When you have built the same kind of project an interviewer asks about, you can connect their question to something you've done and answer with confidence.

Once you choose a course, follow its order. Lessons build on each other. If you skip a long video because it looks boring, you may miss a concept that the next lesson depends on.

Watch outJumping between five courses at once usually means finishing none of them. Pick one good path and finish it.

Tip 5: Do the practicals

This tip matters most, and people skip it most often. Watching videos and reading theory feels productive, but it doesn't give you real experience. Learners who only watch often say: "I don't understand real-time troubleshooting."

Troubleshooting comes from hands-on work:

  • When you do the demo yourself, things break: wrong region, missing IAM permission, a port not open, a typo in YAML.
  • When things break, you investigate and fix them. Real DevOps engineers do exactly this every day.
  • Each fix becomes a story you can tell in an interview: "I hit this error, here's how I found the cause, and here's how I fixed it."

This course has many hands-on lessons, such as the production VPC project, CI with CodeBuild, an e-commerce app on EKS and EKS with Terraform. If you build all of them yourself, you'll have plenty of real problems to talk about.

Interview angleKeep a short "issues log" while you practise: the error, the cause and the fix. Read it before interviews. Troubleshooting stories show real experience better than any definition you can recite. For question practice, see Lesson 29: AWS Interview Q&A.

Where do certifications best fit in your preparation?
A certification checks what you know. Build the knowledge first with concepts, practicals and interview prep, then use the exam to test yourself.
What's the best way to decide which tools to focus on?
Job descriptions show what employers want right now. List the common skills and compare them with what you know.
Why are hands-on practicals so important?
Real problems only show up when you build things yourself. Fixing them gives you troubleshooting skills and stories for interviews.

Key takeaways

  • Learn DevOps fundamentals (Linux, shell, Git, Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform/Ansible) before diving deep into AWS.
  • Certifications check knowledge; take them last, not first.
  • Read job descriptions every day, list the common skills, and study your gaps.
  • Choose a job-focused course and follow it in order without skipping.
  • Do every practical yourself. Breaking and fixing things builds troubleshooting skill and interview stories.
